Maria was nervous about her first big presentation at university. She had only thirty minutes before her slot and felt the clock like a weight on her chest, but she had practiced for days and believed that _practice makes perfect_. She packed slides, printed notes. On the way, the bus was late and her laptop battery warned low, so when she reached the hall she had ten minutes and a printer jam to fix, which made her think she might _go out of the frying-pan, into the fire_. As she walked to the lecture hall, she noticed students chatting as _birds of a feather flock together_, everyone already forming small groups. She started anyway and kept her eyes peeled for the teacher who could stop her for running over time.  
  
Halfway through a slide, the projector flickered, and she had to _bite her tongue_ instead of panicking while she switched to the printed handouts. A student asked a sharp question and someone nearby began _blowing off steam_, but Maria stayed focused and kept her eyes on the prize. When a group member lent her a laptop cable, the moment felt like proof that _many hands make light work_, and they finished the setup fast enough to keep her talk on track. She smiled when a classmate nodded and grinned _like a Cheshire cat_, which calmed her more than she expected.  
  
Later, she met her group to plan the next project. They hit it off immediately, sharing ideas, and Maria noticed that _a bird in the hand is worth two in the bush_—it was better to focus on what they could do now than worry about perfect outcomes. She knew progress took time, so she told herself _Rome was not built in a day_ and that _small crisis teaches resilience_. When she left, she felt tired but proud because even with little time, she learned to trust preparation, teamwork, and that _every cloud has a silver lining_. She promised herself to _cross that bridge when she came to it_ with future challenges.
