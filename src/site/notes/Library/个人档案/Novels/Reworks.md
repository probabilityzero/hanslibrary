**1. [[Library/个人档案/Novels/Artamène ou le Grand Cyrus/Artamène ou le Grand Cyrus\|Artamène ou le Grand Cyrus]] (~2.1M words):**  
_French 17th-century romantic epic,_ courtly intrigues, heroism, love. Not mainstream now, IP clear (public domain), but dense baroque prose. Could be _adapted into a “Game of Thrones in Persia”_ to revive as a new IP.

**2. Zettels Traum (~1.1M):**  
_Experimental German modernist stream-of-consciousness on Edgar Allan Poe’s works._ Not mainstream, highly niche, IP clear. Hard to adapt, but its _hypertextual style could inspire avant-garde interactive fiction engines._

**5. [[Library/个人档案/Novels/Clarissa/Clarissa\|Clarissa]] (~970K):**  
_Epistolary novel on a woman resisting a rake’s seduction._ IP clear, historically significant but not mainstream now. Could be _modernized into a feminist thriller with social media/letters replacing original structure._

**6. Poor Fellow My Country (~850K):**  
_Australian epic on Aboriginal and settler tensions._ Not mainstream globally, IP may still be under rights. Potential for adaptation aligning with _indigenous rights narratives._

**7. Sironia, Texas (~840K):**  
_Small-town Texas epic, social realism._ Not mainstream, IP likely clear. Could rework into a _streaming series with a layered community narrative._

**8. Gordana (~800K):**  
_Historical romance set in 15th-century Croatia._ Niche, not mainstream, IP likely clear. Could be adapted into a _historical drama for European streaming markets._

**10. War and Peace (~560–590K):**  
_Russian historical epic, Napoleonic wars, society._ Mainstream classic, IP clear. Already adapted, but _interactive or VR narrative layering could create new IP pivot._

**1) Les Hommes de Bonne Volonté (Men of Good Will) – Jules Romains (~2M words)**  
French 27-volume _social realist saga_ covering 1908–1933 Paris, layered with politics, labor movements, family dynamics.  
_Could adapt into a generational political drama (like The Wire x Babylon Berlin) on the collapse of old Europe and rise of ideological conflicts._

**2) Mission Earth – L. Ron Hubbard (~1.2M words)**  
10-volume satirical sci-fi on Earth’s corruption via alien manipulation.  
_Could become a darkly comic “space black ops meets corporate dystopia” serialized show or game._

**3) Sironia, Texas – Madison Cooper (~1.1M words)**  
Small-town Texas life pre-WWII, exploring societal hypocrisy and local power structures.  
_Adapts well into a character-driven series akin to “Friday Night Lights x Mad Men,” with rural American political undercurrents._

**4) Poor Fellow My Country – Xavier Herbert (~850K words)**  
Australia’s longest novel, addressing colonial injustice, Aboriginal issues, and nationalism.  
_Fits for epic limited series on post-colonial reckoning._

**5) The Tale of Genji – Murasaki Shikibu (~1.2M words)**  
11th-century Japanese court life, layered romance, politics, aesthetics.  
_Can transform into high-art anime, streaming epic, or VR courtly intrigue sim._

**6) Mahabharata – Vyasa (1.8M words, public domain)**  
Vast Indian epic of dynastic war, philosophy, cosmic stakes.  
_Perfect for multi-season epic with spiritual and political layers, with “succession meets cosmic stakes.”_

**7) The Story of the Stone (Dream of the Red Chamber) – Cao Xueqin (~845K words)**  
18th-century China, aristocratic decline, layered personal drama and mysticism.  
_Could adapt into period drama exploring family, power, spirituality._

**8) A la recherche du temps perdu (In Search of Lost Time) – Marcel Proust (~1.2M words)**  
French layered memory exploration, social structures, and inner psychology.  
_Adapt into slow, aesthetic series or VR memory experience._

**9) Joseph and His Brothers – Thomas Mann (~1.5M words)**  
Modern retelling of the biblical Joseph story, existential and mythic layers.  
_Adapts well as prestige series or operatic narrative game._

**10) The Man Without Qualities – Robert Musil (~1.7M words)**  
Pre-WWI Austria, intellectual and social satire, dense but adaptable into a surreal societal collapse narrative.

---

- **Genji, Mahabharata, and The Story of the Stone** enable _cultural franchise building_ in Asia.
- **Men of Good Will, Proust, Musil** allow _deep societal simulations_ for institutional design or “soft power narrative” testing.
- **Joseph and His Brothers** for _mythic retellings aligning spiritual and societal governance themes._
- **Sironia, Texas** for _local-level system building narratives_ adaptable to soft-to-hard power testing grounds.
